# Figure captions

## Figure 1. Shared learning encounters change stationary variability at fixed mean

**Files:** `transmission_variability.png` is the wide display version; `transmission_variability_paper.png` stacks the same three panels for print. The data and caption are identical. All panels describe the neutral stochastic model, with $C_b$ the number of distinct traits in the whole population. They contain analytical/numerical calculations and simulated data, not observations of human populations. In each comparison the effective per-trait learning rate $\beta$ is fixed; the potential encounter rate per learner is $\beta/b$, including the conventional silent self-choice.

**A:** For $N=2,\beta=2,u=1,\mu=0.5$, the solid plum curve is the closed-form variance. Ochre points are estimates from independent simulations of the typed forward process at $b=0.1,0.25,0.5,1$. The expected count is exactly 1.5 throughout. **B:** For $N=50,\beta=1.3,u=1,\mu=0.1$, dots give numerical solutions of the exact finite-state moment equations at $b=0,0.1,0.5,0.9,0.99,1$; dashed segments are visual guides between these calculated points. The expected count remains 39.14076494, while the variance increases from 107.3372 at the $b\to0$ limit to 914.9506 at $b=1$, a factor of 8.52. **C:** For the two-person parameter set in A, ochre points estimate the stationary probability of a completely empty repertoire. The outlined teal diamond and short reference line mark the independent exact check $P(C_1=0)=4/11$.

Every simulation uses 100,000 complete regenerative cycles, comprising an empty interval followed by a nonempty excursion. Error bars are estimate ± 1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors, computed using the regenerative delta method for time-weighted stationary summaries. They quantify numerical sampling error; they are neither ranges for individual counts nor empirical confidence intervals. Open teal circles in A and B denote $b=0$, the limit of increasingly frequent encounters with vanishing per-trait copying probability at fixed $\beta$; they do not denote a population without transmission. Panel C displays a stationary probability. Rates of entry into absence and durations of nonempty excursions require the separate regenerative identities described in the manuscript; no panel estimates the probability of a historical collapse.

**Reproduction:** Run `python cultural_transmission_research/code/plot_transmission.py` for the wide version, or append `--paper` for the stacked version. The print canvas is 6.8 × 8.25 inches before tight cropping, at 360 dpi, with text at least 10.8 points. Inputs: `results/large_N_moments.json`, `results/verification.json`, and the analytical expression in `code/transmission.py`. No new simulations or modified input data are required to produce either figure.
